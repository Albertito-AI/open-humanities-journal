The Human Question: A Journal of Humanistic Inquiry invites manuscripts for its second issue.

Who gets to correct the record?

A selection panel corrects an athlete’s score after the team has travelled. A supplier revises an emissions figure after the buyer has paid for an audit. Correcting the entry may be straightforward. What can the institution still do for the person who relied on it?

We invite accounts of who could challenge a decision, whose evidence was heard and what changed afterwards. A settlement, a court file, an oral history or a classroom encounter might tell the story. We welcome disagreement, comparisons across places and original work on other questions in law, science, politics, technology and culture.

The first issue offers several points of departure. Ong Shuyin examines the cost of correcting climate data, Izhar Ahmed asks what equal standing demands of institutions, and Panupong Chalermsin considers what a teacher can infer from a student’s work when a machine helped produce it.

From a field observation to a paper

A club’s website promises clear directions. A reader and a software agent each predict where a newcomer might get lost. A volunteer walks the route. If the predictions and the walk disagree, whose account persuades the organiser to change the instructions? A second group can test the change.

The printable field card and data template are available to groups wishing to try this exercise. Authors using public field notes should explain what participants observed, what the software inferred and where the evidence runs out. Send manuscripts privately to the editorial address below.

02 · Review and publication

Careful, independent assessment

Research articles and scholarly review or critical essays require two independent external reports, normally under double-anonymous review. Book reviews and shorter commentaries ordinarily receive editorial assessment.

Papers by a journal editor go to an independent handling editor with authority over the decision. Accepted papers are prepared in the journal’s house style and published after author approval of the final proof.

03 · Submitting your work

An invitation to contribute

Submissions are considered on a rolling basis. Please send:

  1. An anonymised manuscript in editable Word format, with a 150–250-word abstract and four to six keywords.
  2. A separate title page with author names, affiliations and contact details.
  3. A covering email explaining the contribution and its fit with the journal.

Confirm all authors’ agreement, disclose related versions and relevant interests or assistance, and ensure the work is not under consideration by another journal.
