How a paper reaches publication

Submission, correspondence and peer review take place by email. The website holds journal information and the published archive.

  1. 01

    Email your manuscript

    Send the manuscript, title page and a short covering email to the confirmed submission address.

  2. 02

    Editorial assessment

    The editors consider fit, contribution and readiness for external review. The handling editor must be independent of the authors.

  3. 03

    External peer review

    Suitable research articles and scholarly review or critical essays receive two independent external reports, normally under double-anonymous review. The handling editor communicates the decision by email.

  4. 04

    Revision and author approval

    Authors respond to the reviews and revise their papers. Accepted work is prepared in the journal’s house style and returned for proof approval.

  5. 05

    Publication in the open

    The approved PDF and its article details are added to the journal archive for readers to access and share.

Before you send

Your submission package

  • An anonymised manuscript with a title, 150–250-word abstract and four to six keywords.
  • A separate title page with author names, affiliations and contact details.
  • ORCID identifiers, where available.
  • Complete and consistent citations, footnotes and references.
  • A short explanation of the paper’s contribution and its relevance to humanistic inquiry.
  • Relevant interests, funding and assistance disclosed, with all authors’ agreement to submit.

No submission, review or publication fees. Read the author instructions and editorial policies before submitting.

Language & argument

Clarity before ornament.

Use British English, informative headings and a clear account of the paper’s question, approach and contribution. Explain specialist terminology for readers beyond your discipline.

Distinguish evidence, interpretation and speculation. Quotations, factual claims and references should be accurate and traceable.

References & sources

Make the evidence accessible.

Use a consistent citation system appropriate to your discipline. Supply complete references and stable links or identifiers where available.

Use page footnotes only, with full identifying details at first mention and author, short title, earlier note number and pinpoint thereafter. Preserve stable links or identifiers. Legal scholarship follows OSCOLA, fifth edition. Convert author-date citations before publication. Articles end with the conclusion, without a reference list or bibliography.

Tables & figures

Let the material do the work.

Number tables and figures, give them informative captions and identify their sources. Explain abbreviations. Include descriptive text for visual material and obtain any required permissions.

Final publication

A proof the author has checked.

After acceptance, the editors prepare a uniform PDF and ask the authors to check it. Publication follows approval of the final text, author details and applicable licence.

Open access · No author fees

Authors retain copyright. Published articles use CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, allowing noncommercial sharing of the unadapted work with attribution. Read the rights policy for permissions and exceptions. The template is a specimen, with no assigned article identifiers.

Editorial standards · Version 1.0 · 8 November 2026

Clear standards.
Accountable publishing.

The journal is free to read and charges authors no submission, review or publication fees. These documents explain how we assess, prepare and publish work.

President and Editor-in-Chief: Albert Tan Sin Hon, the journal’s founder, based in Singapore. Publisher: The Human Question Project. Editorial and privacy contact: editor@thehumanquestionjournal.com.

Research articles and scholarly review or critical essays require two independent external reports, normally under double-anonymous review. Book reviews, editorials and shorter commentaries are labelled and ordinarily receive editorial assessment. Founder- or editor-authored papers require a qualified independent handling editor with control of the decision.

Authors retain copyright. The standard article licence is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. It permits noncommercial sharing of the unadapted work with attribution; additional uses may require permission. The first issue, 2026 Number 1, is available. Independent preservation deposits are planned, and no preservation-service membership or indexing is claimed.
