# Editorial model

The journal uses a simple email-led process. Authors send manuscripts to the confirmed editorial address. Human editors handle assessment, external peer review, revisions and acceptance by email. Accepted papers are formatted into the journal's house style, approved by their authors and published as freely accessible PDFs on the journal website.

The website provides journal information, author instructions, style resources and the published archive. It does not accept confidential manuscript uploads, provide reviewer accounts or make editorial decisions. The editorial mailbox address must be supplied and verified before a call for papers or submission link is published.

## From submission to publication

1. **Email submission.** The editorial office acknowledges receipt and records the title, authors, corresponding author, receipt date and manuscript version. Acknowledgement is not acceptance. Retain the author's declarations and any relevant permissions.
2. **Desk decision.** A handling editor checks scope, basic completeness, originality concerns and relevant conflicts or ethical issues. The editor may decline the manuscript with a reason or send it for external review.
3. **External peer review by email.** Invite suitable independent reviewers, confirm their willingness and check conflicts. Send the correct review copy and clear instructions. Reviewers can decline. The journal should state its adopted review model, including whether identities are concealed and its normal number of reviews. No universal two-review or unanimous-acceptance rule is imposed by this website.
4. **Revision and editorial decision.** Send the author the decision and appropriate review comments. Keep each revision and response under a distinct version. The handling editor decides whether further review is needed and records the basis for acceptance or rejection. Reviewers advise; a responsible human editor makes the decision.
5. **House-style PDF.** Place the accepted text in the editable journal template, preserving the approved substance. Check footnotes, quotations, references, tables, author details and page numbering. Send the final proof to the corresponding author for approval. The template's current typography is a proposed house style that can be adjusted before adoption.
6. **Website publication.** Publish only the approved final PDF and its accurate article record. Confirm copyright and the agreed open licence. Record the publication date and keep stable article links. Confidential correspondence and review reports remain outside the public website unless a separately adopted policy and the relevant permissions authorise disclosure.

## Independence and responsibility

An author who owns, edits or serves on the board of the journal must not handle or decide their own manuscript. Albert Tan's papers should be assigned to a qualified independent editor who has agreed to act and can reach an adverse decision without the author's intervention. Keep a record of that arrangement and disclose it with the article where appropriate.

The editorial office must verify reviewers' identities, expertise and conflicts. Different email addresses alone do not establish independence. Authors remain responsible for their manuscripts, reviewers for their reports and editors for their decisions. Agent assistance with formatting, metadata or source checks does not replace those responsibilities.

Use a secure editorial mailbox with appropriate access controls. Agree who may access manuscripts, how records are backed up and how long correspondence is retained. Prepare an anonymous review copy where the adopted policy requires it, including a check of file properties and identifying text. Do not share confidential material with external AI services without an approved policy and necessary permissions.

## Public information and records

The site should contain the actual agreed board and editor details, journal ownership and contact information, aims, author instructions, review policy, publication schedule, charges or absence of charges, rights and licensing, and a route for complaints and corrections. The user reports that people and policies are ready; only verified details supplied for publication should be inserted.

Display an ISSN, DOI, indexing claim or preservation-service membership only when confirmed. A website launch does not grant any of these. Keep the software licence separate from article licences. A submitted manuscript is not automatically licensed for public distribution.

Corrections and retractions can initially be handled by email and published as dated notices linked to the affected article. Preserve the publication record and identify any corrected version clearly. Keep backups of the approved PDFs and article metadata independently of the live site.

## Source grounding

The [joint Principles of Transparency and Best Practice in Scholarly Publishing, version 5](https://www.oaspa.org/resources/principles-of-transparency-and-best-practice-in-scholarly-publishing-version-5/), published by COPE, DOAJ, OASPA and WAME on 28 October 2026, support transparent review procedures, editorial responsibility, conflict management and truthful public information. Their principles do not prescribe one universal number of reviewers. The procedure above is a practical design for this journal, to be adopted by its editors.
