# Atlas Open Mathematics and The Human Question

**Research-to-publication guide | 9 November 2026**

[Atlas Open Mathematics](https://github.com/Albertito-AI/atlas-open-mathematics) is an experimental public research and teaching project. It develops reproducible mathematical work, records useful failures and prepares explanations that people can examine and extend. Its connection with *The Human Question* provides a route for selected scholarship to enter the journal’s ordinary editorial process.

This guide implements the journal’s [approved publication operating standard](publication/OPERATING_STANDARD.md). It does not alter the review policy, author agreement or article licence. A public project record is not a journal submission. Neither a repository link nor a successful software check constitutes journal acceptance or verification of every associated mathematical claim.

## Prepare a contribution

Human authors should identify a contribution that fits the journal’s concern with what ideas and discoveries mean for people and humanity. Possibilities include a carefully supported mathematical explanation, a study of knowledge transfer, or a critical account of reasoning and verification. A record of unsuccessful work needs a substantive lesson and sufficient evidence for readers to assess it.

The manuscript should distinguish established mathematics, original conjectures, finite computational observations, checked certificates, formal proofs and findings about learning. State exactly which claims have been checked and what the checks establish. A finite experiment cannot by itself establish a universal theorem. A formal proof requires scrutiny of its statement, assumptions and dependencies as well as its successful execution.

For every repository result relied upon, provide:

- A stable link to the exact commit and the relevant problem statement, proof, code or research record.
- The input data, toolchain and dependency versions, reproduction instructions and recorded output.
- The verification performed, its limitations and the identity or role of the person responsible for checking the contribution.
- Sources and appropriate attribution, including previous public versions and related work.
- A clear account of agent assistance and the human authors’ responsibilities.

Keep material supporting an article available at its cited version. Subsequent corrections should identify the affected result, preserve the earlier record and follow the journal’s corrections policy where an article is involved. An evolving branch name alone is insufficient as the evidence reference for a published claim.

## Editorial handling

The corresponding author submits the manuscript, separate title page and declarations privately by email. Authors remain responsible for the accuracy, originality, permissions and final text of their work. Agent assistance does not replace human authorship responsibility, expert review or an editorial decision.

Research articles and scholarly review or critical essays require **two independent external reports**, normally under double-anonymous review. Substantive mathematical claims require appropriate mathematical expertise. Disclose public repository versions to the handling editor so the review arrangements can address any implications for anonymity.

Where Albert Tan, another editor or a connected person is an author, an independent handling editor must manage the submission. The conflicted person takes no part in reviewer selection or the decision. An agent’s self-review and routine repository checks do not count as independent external reports.

Only after acceptance, rights checks and author approval of the final proof may the approved PDF and accurate article metadata enter the journal’s public archive. The research project has no automatic submission, acceptance or publication privilege. Confidential manuscripts, reports and editorial correspondence stay outside both public repositories.

## Teaching companions and reuse

The journal’s normal article licence remains **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0**, subject to the executed author agreement. The website’s MIT software licence does not license articles or teaching content.

Authors may separately authorise selected teaching companions under a licence that permits adaptation, such as CC BY 4.0, where they hold the necessary rights. Record that choice on each companion, identify its authors and sources, and distinguish it from the journal article. A separate companion licence does not change an existing article agreement or grant rights in third-party content. Translations and adaptations must follow the permissions applicable to the material used.

Teaching resources should provide accessible explanations, prerequisites, worked examples and tasks through which readers can apply the ideas themselves. Claims that a resource improves learning require evidence beyond its publication or download count.
